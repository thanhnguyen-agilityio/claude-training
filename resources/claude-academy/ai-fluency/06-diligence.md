# Lesson 12: Diligence

## A closer look at Diligence (7 min video)

Delegation, Description and Discernment are mostly about effectiveness and efficiency.
**Diligence** covers the ethics and safety side: taking responsibility for how you use AI.

| Part | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Creation | Choose AI systems carefully and think about how you use them (data shared, privacy, security) |
| Transparency | Be honest about AI's role with everyone who needs to know |
| Deployment | Check the AI-assisted output and stand behind it before you use or share it |

- Expectations for disclosure and checking differ between personal, academic and
  professional settings. It's your job to know which ones apply.

## Exercise — write a diligence statement (~14 min)

A **diligence statement** says how AI was used and states that you take responsibility for
the result. (The course's own statement is on the
[course page](https://academy.claude.com/courses/ai-fluency-framework-foundations).)

1. **Reflect on your project**
   - *Creation*: Which AI did you use and why? What data did you share? Any privacy concerns?
   - *Transparency*: Who is the audience, and what disclosure do they expect? What exactly
     did the AI contribute?
   - *Deployment*: How did you check accuracy? What responsibility are you taking?
2. **Draft it with Claude**. Cover: which AI systems, what they contributed, how you reviewed
   the output, your responsibility for the result, and any context-specific rules.
3. **Attach it** to the finished project (footer, appendix or metadata).

A template I can reuse (in my own words):

> I used [AI tool] to help with [tasks]. I reviewed and checked everything it produced, and the
> final result reflects my own understanding and intent. I take full responsibility for its
> content and accuracy.

**Reflect:** Which part of Diligence is hardest for you? How does disclosing AI use change how
others see your work? Which personal rules do you want for responsible AI use?
