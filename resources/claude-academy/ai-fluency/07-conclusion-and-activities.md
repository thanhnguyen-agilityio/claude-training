# Lessons 13–14: Conclusion and additional activities

## Conclusion (6 min video)

- Recap of the 4Ds across Automation, Augmentation and Agency.
- Fluency comes from **practice over time**, not quick mastery.
- Delegation: your expertise and judgment stay the foundation.
- Description: connects your intent to what the AI can do.
- Discernment: critical evaluation within the system's limits.
- Diligence: accountability, transparency, responsibility.
- The best outcomes come when humans and AI build on each other's strengths.
- AI is powerful but not magic. It's only as useful and safe as the way we use it.

- How the 4Ds apply to your own job or field
- What new competencies might matter as AI improves
- Which Ds you seem strong or weak in, judging from the conversation
- Review one of your course exercises to find ways to improve

## Additional activities (self-directed)

**Personal AI fluency plan** (build it with Claude)
1. Rate yourself on each D (novice / developing / confident). Note strengths, gaps, and which
   modes you use most and least.
2. Pick 1–2 competencies to focus on first.
3. Plan activities, a practice schedule, resources and how you'll measure progress.

**Personal prompt and pattern library**
1. Pick 5–10 tasks you do with AI again and again, and write a reusable prompt template with
   placeholders for each.
2. Write down which Description techniques and Discernment checks work for which task types.
3. Keep it organized and searchable, with sample outputs, and update it over time.

**Game night** (cheap practice for precise Description and sharp Discernment)
- *Swap riddles*: have Claude guess and explain its reasoning; nudge its thinking instead of
  giving the answer; then switch roles.
- *Co-op crosswords*: the point is steering its reasoning ("only 5 letters", "the other
  meaning of the word").
- *Word association*: find links among 12–20 random words; flag weak links; apply product,
  process and performance discernment.
- *More*: Twenty Questions, taking turns writing a story, tabletop role-play, explaining a
  concept under tight constraints, making up puzzles (or Project Euler problems for
  programmers).
